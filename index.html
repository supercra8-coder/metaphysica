EXP. 01

The Ship of Theseus

Theseus’ ship is preserved in harbour. Slowly, every plank is replaced with a new one. When the last original plank is gone, is it the same ship?

“The matter has gone. Has the ship?”

Plutarch reports the puzzle; Hobbes added the twist that the old planks, reassembled, would make a second claimant to the name.

EXP. 02

The Block Universe

Treat spacetime as a single four-dimensional object. Horizontal is space; vertical is time. Every dot is an event.

“Is the present a fact about the world, or a fact about you?”

Drag the NOW marker. In the A-theory the present is real and everything else fades; in the B-theory every moment is equally real and “now” works like “here” — true only relative to a speaker.

A-theory. Only the present moment exists. The past has ceased and the future is not yet — the fading is not a limitation of the drawing, it is the claim.

§04 · ONE OR MANY?

Six apples. One redness.

The problem of universals: when you say two things are both red, are you pointing at one thing that both share, or two things that resemble each other? Click an apple, then click redness.

Mode · PLATO

Plato. Redness is one thing — a Form — and the six apples have it by participating in it. The apples are red because the Form is red, not the other way round. Notice what this buys you: “red” means exactly the same thing every time you use it. Notice the cost: you now owe an account of how a physical apple relates to a non-physical Form, and of where that Form is.

On a borrowed question

Heidegger’s famous question — “Why is there something rather than nothing?” — was not his invention. Leibniz posed it in 1714 as the demand for a sufficient reason; Kant had already called it the final question of all metaphysics. Heidegger’s originality lies in the asking, not in the question.
